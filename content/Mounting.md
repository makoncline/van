# Cabinet and equipment mounting

## Current approach

- Use rivnuts in appropriate Sprinter wall ribs for framing, then attach cabinets to that framing.
- Use the factory cargo-floor D-ring anchor locations for structural floor attachment instead of relying on screws into the OEM wood floor.
- Candidate floor interface: bolt a 3/4 in plywood base rail or plate across two or more factory D-ring points, then attach the cabinet to that base. Prefer bolts/threaded inserts through the added plywood for major cabinets when practical; wood screws can be secondary attachment.
- Keep the OEM wood floor in place. It acts as the floor/subfloor and spacer; the structural load path should continue through the factory D-ring anchors into the van structure.

## Evidence and examples

### Mercedes-Benz

The van has option **V43 Wood Flooring**. Mercedes-Benz publishes Body and Equipment Guidelines for the 2019+ C907 Sprinter and states that the factory cargo floor is an integral part of the vehicle structure and should not be removed. The operator manual also says to use several cargo tie-down points and distribute loads among them.

- Upfitter Body and Equipment Guidelines: https://www.mbvans.com/en/upfitter/tech-info/beg
- 2025 Sprinter Operator's Manual: https://www.mbvans.com/content/dam/mb-vans/us/manuals/2025/sprinter/Operators%20Manual.pdf

### Exact plywood-base method

Ellsworth Overland's Expedition Kitchen installation manual gives this as one installation method: cut a **3/4 in plywood base**, bolt it down with the **factory D-ring bolts**, then screw the kitchen to that plywood. This is almost exactly the proposed mounting method.

- Manual: https://static1.squarespace.com/static/63cec4b60315422513356d79/t/65f243e21e0f6418adff172a/1710375912740/Expedition%2BKitchen%2BManual%2Bv1.pdf
- See section 1.3, "Securing to Vehicle," page 4.

### Sprinter owner examples

A Sprinter-Source thread on galley mounting includes one builder who attached a cabinet to the two factory floor tie-down points around the sliding-door area and reported that it was extremely solid. Another builder in the same thread built the cabinet frame over the factory tie-down locations and used longer bolts through a 1 x 2 structural profile. That builder connected the cabinetry together so loads were distributed across all 12 factory tie-down bolts and reported no movement after years of use and washboard roads.

- https://sprinter-source.com/forums/index.php?threads/73327/

DIYvan/Hein also recommends using the OEM D-ring locations to retain the Sprinter floor and identifies the floor threads as M8. Their filler pucks are drilled so an M8 bolt can continue into the OEM floor thread.

- https://diyvan.com/products/sprinter-van-floor-filler-pucks
- https://sprinter-source.com/forums/index.php?threads/95264/

### Commercial upfit example

Ranger Design's Sprinter partition system removes the OEM D-rings and installs its floor bracket by reusing the two D-ring bolts. This is another example of a commercial upfit using the factory tie-down bolt locations as structural mounting points.

- https://manuals.plus/m/b9105ebf672e3f679a86ef0d58e994e399c06d5085cbeed675fde26a5a044988

## Candidate implementation for cabinets

A useful removable interface is a long strip of 3/4 in Baltic-birch plywood or a metal rail that spans at least two factory D-ring locations. Bolt the rail to the OEM points with correctly sized M8 hardware and suitable spacers/washers so the OEM floor is not locally crushed. Attach cabinet bases to this rail and also attach the cabinet/frame to wall ribs with rivnuts. For a heavy kitchenette, prefer through-bolts, T-nuts, threaded inserts, or a metal angle between the cabinet and the added base rail rather than depending only on wood-screw pull-out strength.
